significance statement:

Quantum computers are expected to eventually transform how chemical reactions
are simulated. Grid-based methods are attractive for this because, in principle,
they can represent the changing shape of a molecule's electron cloud as bonds
break and form — but on today's small, qubit-limited hardware, even a basic
obstacle stands in the way: building a compact enough quantum circuit for the
Coulomb interaction between electron and nucleus. This work demonstrates the
first complete grid-based simulation of this interaction run end-to-end on a
quantum computer emulator, using a compact circuit design suited to near-term
devices, verified on simple test cases such as the 1D/2D hydrogen atom. The work
also provides broadly applicable guidelines — for regularizing the Coulomb
singularity and for choosing simulation parameters — that reduce wasted qubits
and circuit depth in grid-based quantum simulations at any scale.
