p.12

The first condition that the time step $\deltat$ must satisfy arises from the
sampling theorem applied to the time evolution. Regarding one step of time
evolution via the Suzuki-Trotter decomposition as a sampling process with
interval $\deltat$, the phase factors $e^{-iE\deltat/\hbar}$ can distinguish
energies only modulo $2\pi\hbar/\deltat$. For the exact propagator this
ambiguity is harmless, because each energy eigencomponent acquires the correct
phase modulo $2\pi$ regardless of $\deltat$; indeed, a global propagator such as
the Chebyshev expansion imposes no upper bound on the time step, and neither
does the Suzuki-Trotter decomposition when $\Hep$ and $\Hek$ commute. For
short-time propagators that factorize the evolution operator into
non-commuting parts, however, the time step is limited: although the
Suzuki-Trotter decomposition (referred to as the split-operator scheme by
Kosloff \cite{Kosloff1988}) is unconditionally stable because each factor is
unitary, a time step for which the phase of the propagator exceeds $2\pi$ over
the energy range represented on the grid is meaningless.
Since the potential term is non-positive and the kinetic term is non-negative,
this energy range is bounded by $\max_{\bm{r}}\abs{\Hep(\bm{r})} +
\max_{\bm{k}}\Hek(\bm{k})$, and the condition reads
\begin{equation}
\left(\max_{\bm{r}}\abs{\Hep(\bm{r})} + \max_{\bm{k}}\Hek(\bm{k})\right)
\frac{\deltat}{\hbar} < 2\pi .
\label{eq:nyquist-limit-total}
\end{equation}
